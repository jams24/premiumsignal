1Market structure: who is in control

Everything starts with swings. Higher highs and higher lows mean buyers are in control. Lower highs and lower lows mean sellers are. A BOS (break of structure) is a close past the last swing in the trend's direction. A CHoCH (change of character) is the first close past a swing against the trend, the first warning that control is changing hands.

2Liquidity and the sweep

Above every obvious high sit stop-losses of shorts and buy orders of breakout traders. Below every obvious low sit longs' stops. SMC calls these pools liquidity. Large players need them to fill size, so price often spikes through a pool and reverses. That spike-and-fail is a sweep (also called a swing failure pattern).

3Sweep or breakout?

The same spike can be the start of a real breakout. The difference is the close. A sweep closes back below the level within a candle or two. A breakout closes above it and stays there. Shorting before the close back below is guessing.

4Supply zone (order block) and fair value gap

A strong drop usually starts where sellers placed big orders. SMC marks the last up candle before that drop as an order block, a supply zone. A drop that fast often leaves a fair value gap (FVG): three candles where the third candle's high never reaches the first candle's low. When price comes back up into these areas, sellers often defend them.

5Premium and discount

Take the last swing from high to low and split it at 50%. Above the middle is premium, where shorts are taken. Below is discount, where SMC traders look for longs. ICT's favourite short entry is the 62–79% retracement, which he calls the optimal trade entry (OTE).

6The full short setup

Now put the pieces together in the order a trader checks them: bearish structure, a bounce into premium or a supply zone, a sweep of the liquidity above, a lower-timeframe CHoCH, then an entry with the stop above the sweep and the target at the liquidity below.

7Timeframes: direction, zone, entry

SMC traders read one move on three charts, each about four times faster than the one above. The slow chart decides the direction, the middle chart finds the zone and the sweep, and the fast chart times the entry. This is called top-down analysis. Lesson 6 already used it: the supply zone came from the higher timeframe and the CHoCH from the lower one.

1 · Slow chart

Direction: which way

  1. Read two things only: the last BOS or CHoCH (who is in control) and where price sits in the last leg (premium or discount).
  2. Shorts when it's bearish and price is in its premium. Longs the other way round.
  3. It changes a few times a week, not every hour. If it flips, stop looking for that side.
2 · Middle chart

Zone: where

  1. Find the supply zone and the liquidity (equal highs, the last high) inside the slow chart's premium.
  2. Wait for a candle to sweep it and close back.
  3. Targets come from here or the slow chart: the start of the bounce, then the leg's low.
3 · Fast chart

Entry: when

  1. Only after the middle chart's sweep candle has closed.
  2. Enter on the first close past the last swing before the sweep (the CHoCH). Stop just past the sweep wick.
  3. Never take direction from this chart. It flips both ways many times an hour.

Which control is which step

  1. Direction line under the chart: the coin's 4H and 1H, the last break and premium or discount. That's step 1.
  2. Structure 15m or 5m (the SMC overlay): the leg, the zone, the liquidity and the sweep. That's step 2. Use 15m for gold and big coins, 5m for fast pump coins.
  3. The 1m candles are step 3. When the watch line says "waiting for a 1m close below …", switch the chart to 1m.
  4. The 1m / 5m / 15m / 1H buttons only change how the candles are drawn. They don't change the overlay.
  5. Each paper trade saves whether the 4H agreed with it (4H ✓ or ✗ in your journal), so your own results show whether it helps you.

What our test says. On 204 coins our bot traded (14 Sep – 13 Nov 2026), the simulator's 15m A+ shorts made −0.09R a trade with the 4H trend and −0.03R against it. A+ longs made −0.21R with the 4H trend and −0.33R against it, the worst group. Random entries did the same with or without the 4H trend. So on our coins, agreeing with the slow chart kept you out of the worst longs. It didn't make the setup profitable.

8Zone retest: the version in the group charts

The NZDJPY, USDCHF, oil and Nikkei charts from the Telegram group use the same SMC idea with fewer steps. Mark the order block at the top of a move (the grey box), wait for a break of structure, then put a sell limit at the box's edge for the first return. The stop goes above the box and the target is the recent low. There's no sweep and no lower-timeframe CHoCH.

Skipping the sweep and the CHoCH gets a better price and a bigger R when the zone holds. When price runs straight through the box, the stop is hit with no warning. Our version waits for confirmation: fewer fills and a worse price, but it skips some of the run-throughs.

What our test says. We tested the zone retest on 18 forex, metal, oil and index markets (Nov 2023 – Oct 2026, spreads in). On the 4H it made −0.04R a trade over 5,017 trades, about the same as random entries (−0.05R). Gold was the one exception: +0.26R on the 4H, and +0.14R on an unseen 2022–24 period, but only with forex-tight spreads. With crypto perp fees it lost (−0.13R). The group's winning screenshots are the trades that worked. Nobody posts the stops.

11Our manual short checklist

Pump coins and big coins need different handling. Pump coins wick through tight stops: in our tests, every stop tighter than 8% lost money on them. Big coins allow a tight stop, but then the fees eat the edge.

Tested · pump coins

WEAK pump short with the 🎯 sweep

  1. The bot posts ENTER on a WEAK pump (pump under 40%).
  2. Mark the 24h high. That's the top, with liquidity above it.
  3. Wait for a 5m candle to wick above the top and close back below it. Allow 2 hours.
  4. If price closes above and holds for 6 candles, the new high becomes the top. Keep waiting.
  5. Short at the close. If that candle closed more than 10 minutes ago, skip it.
  6. Stop 8% above entry, never just above the wick. TPs at −5%, −12%, −20%, −30%.
  7. No sweep in 2 hours: no trade. You will miss some winners this way.
Tested · loses after fees

Big coins with a tight stop

We tested this on a year of 15m candles (Nov 2025 – Nov 2026) for BTC, ETH, BNB, DOGE, ADA and AVAX. The rule: short when a 15m candle sweeps a 1H swing high and closes back below it, with the stop just above the sweep and a target of 1–3R, using Bybit fees.

  1. Tight stop (median 0.44%), with the bearish 4H and premium filters: −0.19R per trade over 1,045 trades. It loses in both halves of the year and on all 6 coins.
  2. Before fees it makes +0.10R per trade. The 0.11% round-trip fee is a quarter of a 0.44% stop, so fees cost 0.29R a trade.
  3. Wider stops (0.6–3%) only get to break-even: +0.05R per trade at 2R, and negative with the next stop setting.
  4. The slower version (4H swing high, 1H trigger) loses too: −0.26R per trade.

A tight stop on a big coin isn't cheap risk: the fee becomes a big part of it. We won't send big-coin signals until a version passes this test.
